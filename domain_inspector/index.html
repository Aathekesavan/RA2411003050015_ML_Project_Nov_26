F1 · technology-fingerprint

Update an outdated component

Medium Sample Finding Hours
What it means
Your site announces PHP 7.4.3. PHP 7.4 stopped getting security fixes on 28 November 2022, so new problems in it won't be fixed.
Evidence
X-Powered-By: PHP/7.4.3
PHP 7.4 — end of life 2022-11-28
Expected
A supported PHP 8 release (8.3 or later), version not announced (expose_php = Off)
Fix
Verify actual running PHP runtime, upgrade to a supported release, and disable version announcement headers (set expose_php = Off).

F2 · cookie-flags

Protect the session cookie

Medium Sample Finding Minutes
What it means
Your homepage sets a session cookie without the Secure and HttpOnly flags. Secure keeps it on encrypted connections; HttpOnly keeps it away from client scripts.
Evidence
Set-Cookie: PHPSESSID=…; path=/
              ↑ no Secure, no HttpOnly
Expected
Set-Cookie: PHPSESSID=…; path=/; Secure; HttpOnly; SameSite=Lax
Fix
Add the Secure and HttpOnly flags (and SameSite=Lax) to session cookies. In PHP: session.cookie_secure = 1 and session.cookie_httponly = 1.

F3 · dns-email-auth

Add a DMARC record for your domain

Low Sample Finding Minutes
What it means
A DMARC record tells receiving mail servers what to do with email that claims to come from your domain, preventing email spoofing.
Evidence
_dmarc.example.com  TXT  (no record)
Expected
_dmarc.example.com TXT "v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@example.com"
Fix
Publish a _dmarc TXT record with p=none to monitor. When reports confirm your real mail passes SPF and DKIM, switch to p=quarantine or p=reject.

F4 · security-headers

Tell browsers to always use HTTPS

Low Sample Finding Minutes
What it means
An HSTS header sends browsers straight to https:// on return visits, protecting against ssl-stripping and downgrade attacks.
Evidence
Strict-Transport-Security: (header absent)
Fix
Send Strict-Transport-Security: max-age=31536000 once all subdomains support HTTPS.

F5 · security-headers

Add a Content Security Policy

Low Sample Finding Days
What it means
A Content Security Policy restricts what scripts and assets pages may load, reducing damage from Cross-Site Scripting (XSS).
Evidence
Content-Security-Policy: (header absent)
Fix
Start with Content-Security-Policy-Report-Only, review violation reports, then enforce.

Questions & Answers

Frequently asked questions.

Need assistance? Contact security@sentinelx.local

What public surfaces does SentinelX inspect?

SentinelX audits what a website publicly advertises to the internet: HTTPS availability and 301 redirection, TLS certificate validity, issuer and protocol version, HTTP response security headers (HSTS, CSP, XFO, XCTO), public DNS email defenses (SPF, DMARC, CAA), cookie attributes, and RFC 9116 security.txt.

Is this check safe for production web servers?

Yes. SentinelX strictly adheres to non-disruptive, read-only requests. It sends a small number (< 10) of polite HTTP and DNS requests identical to an ordinary client browser opening your homepage.

Does SentinelX perform intrusive exploitation or brute-force tests?

No. Intrusive testing—including exploit payloads, password brute-forcing, SQL injection probes, and denial-of-service tests—is strictly prohibited and outside the scope of SentinelX exterior checks.

Why is operator authorization required?

Even though all checks are non-disruptive, SentinelX enforces authorization confirmation to ensure responsible security posture management and prevent unintended audits.

How do I run this standalone website?

Run python server.py in the REPLICA/ folder to launch the standalone local server with full live verified TLS socket and header inspection. You can also open index.html directly in a browser for live DoH DNS checks.

Is this a replacement for a comprehensive manual penetration test?

No. SentinelX provides automated point-in-time posture verification of public configuration and exterior attack surfaces. It complements internal security assessments and source code audits.
